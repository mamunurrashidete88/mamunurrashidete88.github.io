About

I’m a PhD candidate at Deakin University’s School of Information Technology, supervised by Prof. Yong Xiang and Dr. Md Palash Uddin. My thesis, Enhancing Security and Trust in Federated Learning, develops ways for distributed AI systems to keep learning when some participants can’t be trusted: reputation and incentive mechanisms that keep collaboration fair, methods that detect, isolate and recover anomalous clients, and analyses of how much privacy a model leaks as it grows.

I came to research from industry. For 9.5 years I worked at Robi Axiata, a multinational telecom operator in Bangladesh, moving from first-line support for radio, core and IP networks to managing technology operations, and building AI and RPA tools that ran in production. Before Deakin, I completed a Master of Engineering in AI Convergence at Pukyong National University in Busan, where I worked on blockchain-enabled federated learning for IoT intrusion detection.

Research

Three questions about federated learning, plus earlier work on blockchain and multimedia security.

Who should the server trust?

Reputation-based consensus and adaptive incentives that reward honest contribution, and identity schemes that resist Sybil attackers who create many fake clients or lie dormant before striking.

See J1, M1, M3

What happens when clients misbehave?

Trust-aware aggregation that detects anomalous clients, isolates them, and lets them recover, and models that track each client’s behaviour across training sessions rather than one round at a time.

See J2, M2

How much does a shared model leak?

Analysis showing how privacy risk in federated learning scales with a model’s effective dimension, and what that means for building private systems.

See C1
