Research focus

Three questions. More reliable collaboration.

Federated learning keeps raw data with its owners. My work asks what happens when the participants, their updates, or their identities cannot simply be trusted.

Who should be trusted?

I develop reputation and incentive mechanisms that reward reliable contributions while accounting for changing behavior.

What if clients misbehave?

I study how to detect and isolate harmful updates while giving benign, temporarily unstable clients a path to recovery.

Whose identity and data?

I examine deceptive identities and privacy risks that emerge when models learn across many clients and sessions.

Earlier work includes industrial IoT intrusion detection, blockchain systems, and multimedia integrity. Read my background.

About

From network operations to trustworthy AI

I submitted my PhD thesis, Enhancing Security and Trust in Federated Learning, at Deakin University in November 2026. My research spans reputation, anomalous-client recovery, participant identity, and privacy.

Before research, I spent 9.5 years at Robi Axiata in telecommunications. I worked across network operations, analytics, and automation. That experience shaped my interest in AI systems that remain dependable outside controlled experiments.

I hold a Master of Engineering in AI Convergence from Pukyong National University, South Korea, where I worked on blockchain-enabled federated learning for IoT security.
